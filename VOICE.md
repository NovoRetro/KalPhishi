# Voice — how Bathtub Bets talks

**Written 2026-08-16.** A copy audit and rewrite proposal. The taglines are not under
review: they are the best writing in the app and they set the standard everything else
should serve.

## The central finding

The instinct that the copy "sounds like an AI wrote it" is right, but the fix is **not**
to make it funnier. It is to make it **shorter, and to stop explaining**.

Read the app end to end and the tell is not vocabulary, it is *posture*. Almost every
string I wrote does three things a person would not do: it explains itself, it qualifies
itself, and it reassures. "Saved — editable until the lock." "Your picks are always scored
against the real setlist either way." "A part-filled card saves. There is no minimum."
Each is *accurate*, and each is answering a question nobody asked out loud. Stack forty of
them and the app reads like documentation with a game attached.

**The taglines are the comedian. The UI is the straight man.** That is the actual rule.
`TAGLINES` carries 25 deep cuts — Meatstick in Japanese, "You Enjoy My App", "Never Woo A
Wilson" — and it lands *because* the surrounding chrome is quiet. If every string starts
cracking jokes, the taglines stop being a punchline and become wallpaper. So the goal is
not more voice everywhere. It is less copy, in more native words, so the voice we already
have gets room.

## Three rules

1. **Say it once and stop.** Delete the second clause. The one after the em dash is
   usually the AI tell — I use it to pre-empt a question, and pre-empting reads as
   nervous. (This document is allowed to break this rule. The app is not.)
2. **Use the community's nouns.** Not "the show has started" but *showtime*. Not "graded"
   but *scored*, and better, *after the encore*. Not "predictions saved for the open show"
   but *who's in for Friday*. Nobody at a show says "prediction" — they say *called it*.
   Fans say night one, run, tour, lot, bustout, type II, the boys.
3. **Trust them.** This is a closed beta of thirty Phish fans who found their way here
   through a friend's invite link. They do not need "there is no minimum" or "you can
   still change it". They need to know what locks, and when.

**Exempt: the Nerd Zone.** Its whole value is rigour, and its prose should stay long,
careful and hedged — that section is the one place where explaining *is* the product.
Do not Phish-ify statistics.

## Words to retire

| Retire | Use |
|---|---|
| graded | scored, or *after the encore* |
| prediction (in player-facing copy) | your card, your setlist, your ranks, your call |
| the show has started / at the lock | showtime |
| participate / participation | in, as in *4 of 7 in for Friday* |
| resolved | shook out |
| any time before / you can still | *(delete — say when it locks, once)* |

## What is already right

Worth protecting, because a sweeping pass could flatten it: **"Bag it, Tag it"**, **"Ask
Diego?"**, the **🍩 donut free square**, **"envelopes are open"**, the reveal-night section
names (**The chalk**, **Sole calls**, **The steals**), and every one of the 25 taglines.
These are the voice. Everything above is just getting out of their way.
